Kneora prototype

Your recovery, made easier to understand.

Total knee replacement recovery is gradual and different for each person. This guide explains the broad plan and the camera measurements you will see. Your own hospital and physiotherapist’s advice comes first.

The overall picture

Pain, swelling, movement, strength and confidence can improve at different speeds. Large studies show that pain and function often improve quickly at first, then continue more gradually through the first year. An individual camera number does not describe the whole recovery.

Systematic review of 191 studies and 59,667 patients ↗

Outline of the plan

Your programme, stage by stage

These stages organise the prototype. They do not replace your individual rehabilitation plan, and the exercises you have actually been prescribed always take priority.

WEEK 0–1

Settle and activate

Manage swelling, move safely, practise straightening and bending, and wake up the thigh muscles.

WEEK 1–4

Restore movement

Continue range-of-motion work and begin controlled strengthening within the plan given to you.

WEEK 4–8

Build control

Progress strength, balance and everyday movement when your clinical team says it is appropriate.

WEEK 8+

Return to function

Continue building capacity for your own daily activities. Recovery commonly continues for several months.

Measurements in everyday language

What the app’s numbers mean

Knee bend

0° means straight. A larger number means more bend. The app estimates the angle formed by the hip, knee and ankle in a side-on picture.

Hip raise

For a straight leg raise, this estimates how far the whole thigh lifts away from the line of the body.

Toe direction

For a straight leg raise, 0° means the heel-to-toe line points towards the top of a level camera picture. It is a positioning cue, not an ankle assessment.

Repetitions

A repetition is one complete movement away from the resting position and back. An incomplete movement is not counted as zero ability.

Time, pace and hold

These describe how long the session lasted, how quickly complete movements were performed, and how long the leg stayed near the end of a movement.

Tracking quality

This reports how often the camera could see the required landmarks. High visibility is not the same as clinical accuracy.

What recovery usually involves. NHS guidance says full recovery may take several months or longer and recommends following the exercises given by the hospital. Rehabilitation commonly addresses movement, strength and everyday function, but a systematic review found that evidence does not support one universal programme for everyone. Live camera display

Brightness and contrast adjust automatically. The complete camera frame stays visible. Compare Intel depth with a height-based estimate.

Live angles and required landmarks are always shown. They appear while positioning, during the countdown and throughout the exercise. Unavailable or unreliable angles show a dash. The ± value and shaded band describe short-term steadiness, not clinical accuracy.

Only the landmarks needed for the exercise are outlined. Heel slides and seated knee extensions show the operated hip, knee and ankle with the live knee angle. Straight leg raises also show the operated-side shoulder, heel and toe, with live hip-raise and toe-direction angles. A toe reading of 0° means the toes point towards the top of a level camera picture. The other leg, face and unrelated upper-body landmarks are never drawn. The outline is steadied for display; measurements use the original positions.

These displays need the pose model and a clear view of your leg. An unavailable angle is shown as a dash.

Start and finish sessions

Start from your exercise position. Show an open palm towards the camera and hold it for two seconds, or press Start (or the space bar). A five-second Marin countdown gives you time to lower your hand and settle before recording starts. Your palm can be anywhere in the picture and your face does not need to be visible; the Start button works without hand recognition.

To finish, wave again, hold a hand up for three seconds, or select Finish. The app confirms when recording has stopped and you can move from your exercise position.

Spoken guidance uses Marin, an AI-generated voice with a soft, warm British female delivery. The countdown stays visible if audio is unavailable.

MediaPipe detects the hand gesture on this device. Camera access is needed; the microphone is not used.

Your programme, week by week

We are focusing first on heel slides, straight leg raises and seated knee extension, with accurate repetition counting and assessment of movement quality as our development priorities. A repetition is counted each time your joint moves away from its resting angle and comes back. It works for small movements of a few degrees. For straight leg raises the hip angle is shown as well. Movement analysis starts automatically when your recording stops, and the full report appears here when it is ready. Your full report shows repetitions, joint angles, cadence, fastest and slowest repetitions, hold times, movement speeds and total time, with a breakdown of each repetition. You can also replay or download your video. Camera measurements and provisional SLR estimates are not validated clinical scores. Other exercises can be opened as work-in-progress previews while their counting and measurements are developed. The week you are in now is marked from your operation date; do the ones your physiotherapist has given you.

Listen to your knee. Mild discomfort is normal; sharp pain is not. Stop and contact your surgeon if you notice fever, calf pain, spreading numbness or wound drainage. This programme supports your physiotherapist's plan; it does not replace it.

About the app and medical disclaimer

This prototype has not been clinically validated for diagnosis or treatment decisions. Camera estimates and repetition counts can be inaccurate or unavailable. They do not confirm that an exercise is safe or correctly performed, and provisional video estimates are not validated clinical scores.

Only perform exercises your treating clinician has advised. Do not change your rehabilitation plan on the basis of an app result. Stop exercising if you experience sharp pain and seek advice from your clinical team. The app is not an emergency service and sessions are not monitored by a clinician.

The exercise library and week labels provide a general structure, not an individual prescription. Exercises labelled “Work in progress” are available as previews while their counting and measurement features are developed.

Privacy and your data

Cambridge Kinematics does not collect the patient details or exercise records entered into this prototype. Patient details, preferences, activity dates, exercise ticks, questionnaire reminders and results, and saved session results stay in this browser on this device. There is no patient account, central patient database or automatic synchronisation between devices. Clearing browser site data can remove these records, and other people using the same browser profile may be able to view them.

Camera frames are processed on this device. When enabled in Settings, exercise video can be recorded and session files downloaded. Downloaded files are separate from browser storage; keep them securely and share them only with people you choose.

Spoken prompts use generated Marin audio played by this device. The exercise flow does not request microphone access or listen for voice commands.

The website is hosted by GitHub Pages and loads fonts and, when needed, model resources from external providers. These services receive normal web-request information such as your IP address. Local processing does not mean that visiting the website makes no network requests.
