Review a recorded straight leg raise, seated knee extension or heel slide. See joint angles, movement range, repetition timing and relevant clinical references.

Research prototype. Optional scores count only the selected criteria, including knee control. They are not physiotherapist ratings or a recovery score. This tool cannot assess pain, muscle strength, passive extension or whether the exercise is appropriate for you.

1. Choose your recording

Your video is processed in this browser and is not uploaded. The tracking software and model are included locally.

Use a fixed camera looking squarely at your side, with shoulder, hip, knee and ankle visible. No starting pause is needed. The analyser estimates the starting position from the recording. Include complete movements that return to that position. Use a clip under five minutes.

2. Optional settings

Choose the exercise and intended leg. SLR targets are optional. Clinical reference information appears with the results; it does not change your prescribed exercise.

A lower visibility threshold accepts less certain landmarks and may increase tracking errors. Thresholds used for movement detection and optional SLR criteria are prototype settings, not validated clinical cut-offs.

Frame quality checks

MediaPipe landmarks are checked against the visibility threshold shown in this report, presence when supplied (at least 0.2), image bounds and segment size. These are engineering thresholds, not validated accuracy limits. Rejected measurements are excluded from statistics; a missing shoulder can still leave a usable knee measurement. Gaps are not filled in or scored as completed movements.

Observed joint measurements

These values summarise the frames that could be measured, even if no complete repetitions were found. Approximate hip flexion is 180° minus the shoulder–hip–knee angle, a 2D trunk-relative estimate. It is affected by camera view and trunk movement. Observed peaks are not proof of your maximum range of motion.

Clinician assessment only: Quadriceps Activation Battery

The QAB was studied in 162 patients after total knee arthroplasty, with early assessment around four days after surgery. This panel records a clinician’s assessment using the published test protocol and scoring criteria (Figure 1). It is not an automated validated video score.

Each test is rated 0–2 using its specific published criteria. A total out of 6 is shown only when all three tests are assessed. Do not infer missing tests from this video or use the camera’s bend threshold as the clinical rubric.

QAB total: not assessed. Clinical test results are required.

Evidence for the clinical battery does not validate camera scoring or an isolated straight leg raise score. The original study also does not establish applicability to every diagnosis or recovery stage.

Final SLR assessment

The published test starts lying down at the current extension limit, with the opposite knee at 90°, and asks for a heel lift of 2 feet. This describes the study protocol, not a new exercise prescription. The component score is 0–2; the full three-test QAB is 0–6. A video estimate is not a validated application of the clinical scale.
