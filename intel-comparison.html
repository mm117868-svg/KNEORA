Evidence and assumptions

Simon et al., 2023: 4,200 participants. We calculate participant-weighted means from the published height groups within 3 cm of the entered height (Tables 3 and 4). At least 20 reference observations are required. We do not reverse the paper’s height-prediction equations.

The study measured radiographic bone lengths, which differ from the camera’s surface landmarks. The fit makes small depth adjustments along the observed viewing rays using those lengths as soft constraints. It does not locate internal joint centres. The 15 mm depth penalty, 10% length penalty and 30 mm axial adjustment limit are experimental settings, not clinical error bounds. No individual prediction interval is available from the grouped data. These results are separate from exercise records.

How these estimates work

Both methods use the same detected image landmarks. Intel depth supplies visible surface coordinates. The separate height model uses thigh length 24.5% and shank length 24.6% of height, with calibrated camera intrinsics and a planar assumption. It does not use Intel depth to set its scale. These are population-based engineering priors, not individual measurements. Height scales the estimated positions and distance; it does not change the image-plane knee angle. Neither method sees the internal joint centre.

Results are withheld for inadequate landmark visibility, missing or inconsistent depth, and strongly inconsistent image scale. The methods remain separate and are not averaged. Small differences may reflect shared landmark errors. Larger differences can arise from anatomy, pose, clothing, tracking or depth errors.
